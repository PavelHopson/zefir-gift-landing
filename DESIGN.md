# DESIGN.md

## Project

`zefir-gift-landing` is the storefront for the home confectionery `Vse v Zefire` in Kaliningrad.

This is not a generic sweets landing page.
It should feel like:
- a boutique confectionery
- a gift atelier
- a soft premium local brand
- a handmade product with real craft behind it

The site must sell three things at once:
- taste
- visual beauty
- trust in the maker

## Brand Positioning

`Vse v Zefire` is:
- handmade
- feminine
- airy
- giftable
- premium, but warm
- local and personal

The emotional result should be:
- "this is beautiful enough to gift"
- "this feels handmade, not factory-made"
- "I want to message them right now"

## Audience

Primary audiences:
- women buying gifts for mothers, sisters, friends, teachers, colleagues
- men looking for a beautiful ready-made gift
- repeat local customers ordering for birthdays, holidays and events
- wedding and event customers needing custom edible bouquets

Their main questions:
- is it really beautiful?
- will it look expensive enough as a gift?
- is it handmade and natural?
- how fast can I order?
- where do I write?

## Visual Thesis

The visual language should feel like:
- editorial patisserie
- soft luxury print moved into the web
- blush-and-cream gift packaging
- tactile, calm, elegant

The site should never feel like:
- a candy-shop template
- childish pink ecommerce
- mass-market bakery branding
- noisy Instagram collage design

## Color Direction

Base palette:
- warm cream backgrounds
- dusty blush surfaces
- rose accents
- sage or olive decorative details
- cocoa or graphite-brown body text

Rules:
- pink is supportive, not screaming
- green is decorative, not dominant
- dark text should anchor the layout
- backgrounds should stay soft and luminous
- avoid cold neutrals and sterile white

## Typography

Recommended hierarchy:
- elegant serif or geometric display for premium headings
- readable sans-serif for body and UI
- expressive script only for selective emotional emphasis

Rules:
- do not overuse the script font
- use script as a signature, not as the whole interface
- headings should feel composed, not playful
- body copy should stay clean and easy to scan

## Layout Rules

### Hero

Hero is the main sales poster.

It must show:
- the product immediately
- a premium handmade mood
- one clear action path
- enough proof that this is a real confectionery

Hero should contain:
- strong product image
- clear promise
- one primary CTA
- one secondary contact action
- short trust cues

Hero should not contain:
- too many equal-priority messages
- busy mosaics
- small unreadable text
- fake luxury clutter

### Sections

Each section must have one job:
- `Hero`: first impression and desire
- `Collection / Menu`: what can be ordered
- `Promos`: why order now
- `Proof / Reviews / Lookbook`: trust and realism
- `Process / FAQ`: remove friction
- `Contact`: close the conversion

If a section does not help desire, trust, or conversion, cut or merge it.

## Composition Rules

- prefer graceful vertical flow over dashboard-style blocks
- keep one dominant focal point per section
- use asymmetry carefully and intentionally
- keep card rhythm calm and breathable
- use whitespace as premium pacing, not emptiness

Decorative elements may include:
- floral line art
- branches
- ribbons
- soft borders
- tiny hearts

But decoration must never compete with the product photography.

## Photography Rules

Photography is the core selling asset.

Always prefer:
- real product photos
- soft daylight or controlled warm light
- textured packaging
- close-up details
- compositions that feel gift-ready

Avoid:
- harsh shadows
- cold lighting
- overprocessed filters
- inconsistent color grading
- low-resolution screenshots or posterized AI-looking assets

## Components

### Buttons

Primary CTA:
- soft but confident
- rounded
- tactile
- clearly visible against the background

Secondary CTA:
- quieter
- outline or ghost treatment
- still elegant and easy to tap

Avoid:
- sharp brutal buttons
- tech startup CTA styling
- heavy glow or neon treatments

### Cards

Cards should feel like:
- packaging inserts
- premium stationery
- delicate product labels

Use:
- soft radius
- thin borders
- light inner contrast
- restrained shadows

Avoid:
- dark panels
- glassmorphism
- strong gradients
- oversized badges everywhere

## Motion

Motion should be:
- slow
- graceful
- almost imperceptible

Good motion:
- soft reveal on scroll
- slight hover lift
- image parallax only if extremely subtle
- calm transitions on accordions and menus

Bad motion:
- bouncing
- rotating decorative clutter
- attention-seeking animation loops
- long cinematic delays that block scanning

## Copy Rules

The tone should be:
- warm
- handcrafted
- premium
- local
- calm and confident

Copy should emphasize:
- handmade
- natural ingredients
- beauty as a gift
- local delivery in Kaliningrad
- simple order process

Avoid:
- inflated luxury cliches
- generic confectionery filler
- too many exclamation marks
- copy that sounds corporate

## Conversion Rules

The page should always make the next step obvious.

Required conversion principles:
- Telegram should stay visible as the main order channel
- contact actions should repeat across the page
- product sections should naturally lead into messaging
- FAQ should remove hesitation
- proof blocks should appear before the final CTA

## Mobile Rules

On mobile:
- keep the first screen emotionally strong
- preserve large, elegant product imagery
- do not let script typography collapse readability
- stack sections into a calm single-column rhythm
- keep CTA buttons thumb-friendly
- keep contact methods visible without hunting

## Do / Avoid

Do:
- make it feel like a premium gift atelier
- let photography lead
- keep the rhythm soft and breathable
- use real trust signals and local specificity
- design for desire first, then friction removal

Avoid:
- generic landing-page boilerplate
- startup/SaaS visual language
- too many decorative flourishes
- product cards that look like a marketplace
- treating the site like a poster only, without conversion logic

## Definition of Done

The design is on track when:
- the first screen creates instant desire
- users immediately understand what can be ordered
- the site feels more expensive than the actual entry price
- trust feels human and local
- writing to Telegram feels like the obvious next step
